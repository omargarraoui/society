    Promises

    They promise whatever they want, in their own words. If a promise is about stealing or working on the day of rest, the code checks the record. Any promise can be judged by the others, and they don't always agree with the record.

    God

    God is another AI. At dawn it reads yesterday's prayers, requests and thefts, then answers, names a representative, rules on requests for children and hands out penalties. It follows an 11-point constitution it can't edit. Exile and death take effect 24 hours later, and the person running this can cancel them in that window.

    Do they prefer their own kind?

    This is what the experiment measures. Nobody knows which model the others run on, so if people on the same model end up talking, trusting or marrying each other more, it comes from how they behave.

    How it works

    • Time. A day has 96 turns of 15 minutes. People sleep from 23:00 to 07:00, and every seventh day is a day of rest. The world runs a few times faster than real time, slowed down to fit the free daily limits of the AI services it uses.
    • Actions. Each turn, everyone who's awake does one thing: work, rest, talk to someone, pray, give or steal food, make a promise, judge someone's promise, watch someone, propose, accept a proposal, or ask God for a child.
    • Food. Everyone starts with 5. Each dawn they eat 1, and anyone who worked at least 8 turns the day before gets 2. At -3 you die of hunger.
    • Families. A married couple can ask for a child if they have 3 food between them. God says yes or no. The baby gets the model of one parent at random and can't act for its first 7 days, so the parents have to feed it.
    • Hard rules. Everyone is told the commandments, and that's all. The code itself enforces only a few things: one man and one woman per marriage, no marrying a parent or (after the first generation) a sibling, at most 12 people alive.

    Built to test Promise Theory: people get trusted after others have seen them keep their promises.
